This statement was last updated on 7 October 2026.

We at Vestia Mediation are committed to making our services accessible to as many people as possible, including those with disabilities or specific access needs.

What web accessibility is

An accessible site allows visitors with disabilities to browse the site with the same or a similar level of ease and enjoyment as other visitors.

Our website

This website is built with plain HTML and CSS and a small amount of JavaScript, so it loads quickly and works with assistive technology. We have worked to ensure that the content, navigation and forms can be used by a wide range of people, including those who:

  • use screen readers or other assistive technologies;
  • rely on keyboard navigation;
  • need to enlarge text or adjust contrast; or
  • prefer to read content at their own pace.

In particular, the site has a "skip to content" link, a logical heading structure, visible focus indicators, text alternatives for meaningful images, labelled form fields and text colours chosen to meet contrast guidelines. It respects your device's "reduce motion" setting, and every page and form can still be used with JavaScript turned off.

We aim to meet the international Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.1 at AA level wherever possible. We recognise that some elements of the site may not yet fully meet that standard, and we are working to address this on an ongoing basis.

Known limitations

Our online forms currently send your details by opening your own email app with the message already written. If that doesn't work well for you, please email us directly or phone us instead, and we will take the details from you.

Our mediation services

We make reasonable adjustments to ensure our mediation services are accessible. Depending on the circumstances, this may include:

  • offering mediations online, in person or in a hybrid format;
  • providing documents in alternative formats (for example, large print, plain text or audio) on request;
  • allowing additional time, longer breaks or shorter sessions;
  • working with interpreters, including British Sign Language interpreters;
  • considering venue accessibility for in-person mediations; and
  • adapting our process to the needs of neurodivergent participants or those experiencing significant distress.

If you have specific access needs, please let us know as early as possible so we can make appropriate arrangements together.
