Pre-mediation questionnaire

Each party completes this before the mediation day. It gives your mediator a head start: what has happened, what matters to you, and anything that would help them run the day well.

Confidential to the mediator. Without prejudice.

All information you provide is confidential and will only be shared with the mediator. We will not pass it to the other side, or to anyone else, without your permission, except where our Mediation Agreement allows it (for example, where the law requires disclosure). It is also without prejudice: it can't be referred to or relied on in court if the dispute doesn't settle, unless it would have been admissible anyway.

Take your time. Short answers are fine, and you can leave out anything you'd rather discuss with the mediator directly. Questions marked as optional can be skipped. If you're a solicitor completing this for a client, please answer for your client.

Prefer to fill it in offline? Download a blank copy as a Word document and email it to us when it's done.

When you've finished, press "Send my answers" and choose how to send them: written out in an email from your own email app, or saved as a Word document that you attach to an email yourself. Either way, nothing is stored on this website: your answers go only from your own device to our inbox. Email isn't encrypted end to end, so if you'd prefer another way to send this, or need to send documents securely, ask us and we'll arrange it.

1. About you

Your role in the dispute
For example landlord and tenant, neighbours, business partners or family members.

2. The mediation

Evenings and weekends are possible on request.
Online, in person or hybrid, and where, if in person.
Names and roles, including anyone coming to support you. Everyone attending needs to be named in advance.

3. The dispute

A line is enough, for example "dilapidations claim at the end of a commercial lease".
What has happened, in your own words. A few paragraphs is plenty.

4. Your position and what matters to you

Include anything that isn't about money, such as an apology, a timetable for works, or keeping a working relationship.
The needs or concerns driving your position, for example financial security, reputation, certainty, or an ongoing relationship.

5. Previous attempts to resolve it

This section stays with the mediator.

Amounts, terms and dates if you have them.
For example direct negotiation, solicitors' correspondence or a previous mediation.

6. Outcomes

Also for the mediator only. You don't have to answer these now if you'd rather talk them through.

7. Anything the mediator should know

Are you comfortable meeting the other side in a joint session? Many mediations happen mostly or entirely in separate rooms. There's no wrong answer.
Interpreters (including BSL), extra breaks, venue access, large print or anything else that would help.
For example tensions between the people involved, topics to avoid in a joint session, health or wellbeing concerns, or any worries about safety.

8. Documents and consent

For example correspondence, contracts, leases or court documents. Please send copies separately by email. If you'd like to prepare a short mediation summary you're welcome to, but it isn't essential.

Next, you can choose to send your answers in an email or save them as a Word document.
